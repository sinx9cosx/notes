# 原文

The evolution of abstract art in the early twentieth century marks a pivotal shift in the perception and purpose of artwork. Abstract artists often seek to <mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">represent</mark> the<mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;"> essential</mark> qualities <mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">of</mark> emotions <mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">associated</mark> with <mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">the</mark> subject, <mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">focusing</mark> on <mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">mood</mark> and <mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">movement</mark>, as <mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">well</mark> as <mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">symbolism</mark>, rather than realistic detail. The abstract art movement broke away from traditional ideas about art, prompting viewers to think more deeply about what they saw. As artists explored new methods of expression, they revolutionized how people interact with art, creating a conversation between artist and audience.
