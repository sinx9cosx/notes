# 原文改写

I agree that sometimes products can be expensive when they first come out. But as the technology develops, the price usually drops. For example, the latest version of the iPhone is so expensive that I won't buy it right away. I only buy it when the price goes down to save money.

Additionally, the latest products can have bugs that need to be fixed. I have bought a lot of the latest software and games when they came out, and it's typical to find them inconvenient. But with the feedback from users, the software becomes smoother and smoother.

So I agree with Claire's feeling that giving feedback can guide improvements to a product. But for me, I think it's a bit annoying and time-consuming. I prefer complete and perfect products instead of the latest ones.

# 满分作文

I agree with Claire that early adopters can shape a product, but I want to highlight a different advantage: early adopters gain a real head start in skill and knowledge. When a new tool or platform appears, the people who learn it first often become the ones others turn to for help. For example, when the design software Figma first launched, the designers who adopted it early learned its collaborative features before most of their peers. Within a year, many of them were leading workshops and getting hired specifically because they already knew the tool that studios were starting to demand. This advantage is not just about saving money or feeling influential; it is about positioning. Claire focuses on the emotional reward of giving feedback, but the practical payoff of being first is that a person builds expertise while the technology is still scarce. That expertise can translate directly into career opportunities, which is a benefit that lasts far longer than any launch discount.
