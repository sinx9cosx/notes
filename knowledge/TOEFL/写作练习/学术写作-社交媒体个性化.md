# 题目

Your professor is teaching a class on media. Write a post responding to the professor's question.

Social media platforms often use algorithms, which are sets of rules computers follow to make decisions, to show users content they might like or find interesting. This customization means everyone's experience is unique, but it also raises concerns. Some argue it helps users find what they enjoy faster, while others worry it might limit exposure to diverse perspectives and create echo chambers. What do you think? Does the customization of social media through algorithms have negative effects?

Claire:

I believe overreliance on algorithms can negatively affect our online experiences. It limits the joy of stumbling upon new content randomly. This organic discovery often leads to learning about diverse topics and viewpoints, which algorithms might filter out, creating a narrow view of the world.

Andrew:

Personalization algorithms on social media are beneficial, especially for small creators. They help these groups gain visibility among users who have shown interest in similar content. This customization enables users to discover new content they're likely to enjoy, supporting smaller creators who might otherwise be overlooked.

# 回答（4/5）

I agree with Andrew' view that personalization algorithms on social media are helpful for people find someone with like mind. As for me, I love animes. So I often scan contents about new animes, and I discover a people who have the same taste . We become good friends then. 
In addition, it helps people to avoid the contents they're not interested in. That saves people's time and energy. If every kind of contents are read by us, we may feel exhausted about huge numbers of information. 
As for Claire's view, some people will be limited. But as long as you find information actively, you can see various viewpoints and topics. So it depends. For example, if I want to find some classic books to read, I will search it online and see people's advice. That really broadens my horizens.

# 原文改写

I agree with Andrew's view that personalization algorithms on social media are helpful for people to find others with like-minded interests. As for me, I love anime. So I often scan content about new anime, and I discovered a person who has the same taste as me. We **have become** good friends since then.
In addition, it helps people avoid the content they are not interested in. That saves people's time and energy. If we read every kind of content, we might feel exhausted by **a huge amount of information**.
As for Claire's view, some people may be limited by it. But as long as you actively seek information, you can see various viewpoints and topics. So it depends. For example, if I want to find some classic books to read, I will **search for** them online and read people's advice. That really broadens my **horizons**.

# 范文

I agree with Claire that algorithmic customization has real negative effects, but I would push the point further: the harm is not just missed serendipity, it is the quiet narrowing of what counts as normal. When a platform learns that a user lingers on one political viewpoint, it feeds more of the same, and the feed begins to look like a mirror rather than a window. A 2021 study of YouTube recommendations found that users who watched a single climate-skeptic video were soon shown a stream of similar content, with little exposure to mainstream climate science. This is not a neutral shortcut to enjoyment; it is a feedback loop that rewards engagement over accuracy. Andrew is right that small creators benefit, but that benefit comes at a cost. The same mechanism that surfaces a niche artist also buries the unfamiliar perspective that might challenge a user's assumptions. Customization is efficient, but efficiency is not the same as a healthy public conversation.
