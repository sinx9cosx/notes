I don't think requiring children to practice for a certain amount of time each day is an effective way to help them develop a skill. As Andrew says, encouraging children and praising them is a better way to motivate them. Also, children may need flexibility because they are still on the way to finding their interests. For example, I was sent to learn painting when I was a child, but I struggled with the classes every week. This kind of fixed structure is not fit for everyone.

In addition, I think following one's interest is a better way to develop a skill, because children will have more motivation to do something they are interested in. For instance, although I struggled with painting, I was interested in playing badminton. I was eager to go to the classes, and I would practice my skills with my friends even after the classes. For children, it is also easier to keep on practicing the things they love. In short, instead of forcing a fixed amount of practice time, parents should help children discover what they enjoy, since genuine interest leads to more consistent and willing practice.
