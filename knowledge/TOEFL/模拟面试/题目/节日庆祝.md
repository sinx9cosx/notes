## 题目1

Q1: Do you celebrate holidays with your family, friends, or both? How often?

 I'd say both, but in different ways. With my friends, I travel during the national holidays — like National Day or May Day —  maybe two or three short trips a year. With my family, the big one is the Spring Festival. That's the only time of year when everyone — my parents, grandparents, uncles and aunts — gets together, so it's a very precious **memory** for me.

## 题目2

Q2: Describe your favorite holiday celebration. What do you do?

My favorite is definitely the Spring Festival. The highlight is making dumplings with the whole family.  We all gather in my grandma's kitchen — some of us roll the dough, others prepare the filling, and we talk and laugh the whole time. My grandma always hides a coin in one of the dumplings, and whoever finds it is supposed to have good luck for the whole year. It's a small tradition, but it makes the celebration feel really special.

## 题目3

Q3: A memorable special event — what made it meaningful?

A particularly memorable one was last National Day, when I took a trip to Nanjing with my friends. We also met some high school classmates there who now study in different cities. We went to lots of places — parks, the Confucius Temple area — and walked the whole day, so I was completely exhausted. But at night we sat together and talked for hours about our new lives. That's what made it unforgettable: not the sightseeing, but catching up with old friends.

## 题目4

Q4: Are traditional cultural observances becoming less important?

I disagree. I think traditional celebrations still matter, because they give us a sense of belonging that modern life can't replace. For example, one year I was too busy to go home for the Spring Festival, so I just video-called my family on New Year's Eve. Even though we could see each other, I felt uneasy and lonely. But when I finally got home days later, my family welcomed me warmly and even saved dumplings for me — I immediately felt at home again.  That experience showed me that the Spring Festival reunion isn't just an old custom; it carries real emotional meaning, so I don't believe traditions like this will fade away.
